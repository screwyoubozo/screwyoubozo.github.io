To be and not to be (this is not a question)

The title is a bit misleading because some of the following comparisons are not necessarily true opposites. Hah! Thought it'd be "to be or not to be?"

What does "to be and not to be" mean?

I find that many times, people tell me that I'm this or that. A lot. But then their subconscious actions show that they don't really say what they believe in.

At the same time, I also find that I may claim to be something that I'm not, but sometimes my actions convince me of what I'm not.

These are best described with examples, if the above statements do not make as much sense.

Experienced and inexperienced

If you have difficulty following along, please open up a map like OpenStreetMap, Google Maps, etc.

Experienced

Ever since... I was 7 years old? Maybe 5? I mainly had the responsibility of helping my family navigate for road trips, or even for a simple point-A-to-point-B. This was kinda nice because I always enjoyed doomscrolling on maps or Wikipedia or long-form YouTube, but that's beside the point. I also found numbering systems of streets and addresses to be quite interesting. This also extends to understanding how mileposts and exit numbers work.

If an address was given to me, such as, 9200 Bolsa Avenue, Westminster, CA 92683, without having to look at a map, I can tell you that that place is located on the southeast corner of Bolsa Avenue and Magnolia Street. Why? Because Magnolia Street is where the "9000" coordinate starts (and increases east for a mile to Brookhurst Street with the "10000" coordinate), and even numbered addresses are on the south side of the street in Westminster. (However, this also has to do with how I've been to this area so many times. If I was dropped in the middle of San Diego, I would need a map to verify my findings, but I could relatively quickly figure out how the grid system works.)

I also know the way from the Phoenix area to Albuquerque by heart: take AZ-87 north to Payson, AZ-260 east until AZ-277 (around milepost 306), AZ-377 whole length, AZ-77 to Holbrook, then coast along I-40 east. I have been along that drive at least ten times (twenty if you count the way back). Most times, the one who is driving (i.e. my mum) would ask me about where to turn or exit.

Inexperienced

Now, when the rest of the family goes along to Albuquerque, my uncle is responsible for navigating using a Garmin GPS with live navigation. By that time (I think it was in 2017?), I have already tread that part at least 8 times. Now, despite having live navigation active, he misses the turn onto AZ-277 and proceeds to take AZ-260 to Show Low. Not more than a minute passed before I told him to make a U-turn. However, he insisted on following the live navigation. (To add insult to injury, every time he riddled his children, my sister, and myself, whenever I was the last to say "I don't know," he would always respond with "But you're smart, you can figure this out!") I had told him that from experience, it was faster to U-turn and get back onto the right path because continuing on the wrong road would have us travel on surface highways. Even my mum tried to get him to U-turn. So much for being experienced.

Ultimately, we continued onto US-60 into Show Low. It was incredibly dark outside, and there weren't as many heavily populated gas stations unlike the I-40 motorway. It ultimately took us 4 hours longer than if we U-turned. I hope everyone in the car understands that arriving at sunset and midnight are two very different things, and someone as young as me is just as cognisant of that.

This is to say, even those who call me smart, experienced, etc. may subconsciously think that I am stupid, inexperienced, etc. (To add insult to injury, even I have been gaslighting myself into thinking that I'm not as impressive as my research seniors had thought of me when they invited me to join because I effectively got nothing done for five months. That's a story for another time.)

A word of caution

Experience does not necessarily translate into correctness. In a similar manner, age does not necessarily translate into experience. Even I, the one writing this soapbox, am only aware of but an infinitesimal amount of wisdom (for lack of a better term).

Mean and kind

Here's a fun one. Being nice and being kind often are conflated as one and the same. They are very much not.

Suppose you have picky children who don't want to eat vegetables. If you are nice, you would acquiesce and let them eat candy (effectively, no vegetables) instead. However, being kind is looking out for your children and making sure they are healthy, so as mean as you may have to be, you make them eat their vegetables.

It is for this reason that I gaslight my students. Failure is a very effective means of teaching. But of great significance, it also teaches them that my word-of-mouth, even with authority, is not to be accepted unreservedly. A side note, while it is amusing to me to see students confused, confusion means that there is some clashing thought and analysis going on. Hopefully.

Of course, I don't subscribe to the idea of bullying or putting down others just to make myself feel better (or egotistical). Being kind to myself means knowing to pursue what is significant to me and repudiating such mundane matters. Being kind means not being afraid to sound mean for others' benefit. Stroking my ego by putting others down is not of benefit for anyone, including myself; i.e. a pure waste of energy.

I don't have much to write about this. You should get to know me.

Together and lonely

Loneliness is an unpleasant feeling that occurs as a result of being alone. However, whenever I am alone and only aware of my existence, there is not much of a problem. More often than not, solitude means not having to deal with the chaos outside. But what if I say that from my experience, loneliness is only possible when you are not alone?

I find it quite difficult to interact with most others around me. (I wonder if this has to do with being autistic.) Sure, I am able to get along with others without much issue. I can have interests in certain video games (for example), just like other people. More often than not, these others that I interact with have other friends that they are close with, and their interests do not align with mine. Among circles of friends in a cohort of students, I would consider myself a vagrant, accepted by everyone universally, but there persists a hard-to-describe feeling that I do not belong to any camp. If I want to feel included, I have to weasel my way in, and such an approach will not magically make others assimilate to my interests.

It is also for this reason that I do not like third-wheeling. Only when I am considered an equal will I be able to find such an idea palatable, and "being an equal" in such a case is far too impractical to implement.

Ideally, friendships that start from the ground up, where everyone (including myself) is willing to do whatever the hell just for the sake of hanging out (like drinking) and everyone feels included, are the ones that I am able to last. Feeling included is quite difficult because I keep to myself for fear of making things all about myself or ruining the harmony which has been established by others. (I was told a lot by my sister many moons ago that I make everything about myself, so I ought to shut up such as not to sound like a narcissist or alien; besides, is there a chance in hell that anyone will have any experience adjacent to mine?)

The only way for me to completely rid this unpleasant feeling of "loneliness" is not to have any friends or interpersonal interaction at all. I am cognisant that such an approach has far too many fundamental flaws.
