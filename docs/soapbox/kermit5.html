The art of self-immolation (part 5 of ?): capital punishment

This title is a bit misleading because I don't describe self-harm or lethal techinques. Seriously, please reach out to a confidant or exit this page if you are planning such things.

The art of self-affirmation

The amount of times that I have considered putting myself out of commission permanently continues to increase by the day. Sometimes, life doesn't feel worth living. At least for me, some motivators thereof would be the cessation of imposing a burden on others, being unable to value myself for who I am, or maybe other reasons that I can't retrieve from my subconscious.

And yet, I can also imagine a pursuit of a PhD researching computer architecture, security, and education, and I am surrounded by wonderful people (or at the very least, people that are willing to work with me). In spite of this, I sometimes see how little I have accomplished (going back to valuing myself very little). I can only place my hope that the other part of me remains suppressed.

This is where the other part of me would claim that it's easier and less anguish to take myself out right there and right now. By the way, I don't think I have dissasociative identity disorder; I ought not to LARP in such a way. "You attack me and tie me up? Blame your brain! You are your fucking brain, you dipshit!" ~ Mary Kline, from Backrooms

But regardless of which mask I wear today, or any given moment, positive self-affirmation will discourage me from kicking my own bucket.

The art of necessity

There are far too many things that I don't want to do - maybe things like chores, essay writing, or filling someone else's incompetencies. No matter what I want or don't want, I still have to get them done. While this algorithm suppresses any opportunity for negative and self-harm thoughts. Eventually, once I'm freed from these necessities, I will start to ask myself if I'm satisfied with being a slave to someone else's will? Or how miserable I've been living?
